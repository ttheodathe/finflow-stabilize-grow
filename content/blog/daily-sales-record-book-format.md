# Daily Sales Record Book Format: What to Write and How to Use It

A daily sales record book should make it easy to answer four questions: what did the business sell, how much was charged, how much has been received, and what remains unpaid?

The format does not need to be complicated. A small shop may begin with a paper register, while a business with more products or several people recording transactions may prefer a spreadsheet or accounting system. What matters is that records are complete, consistent, and easy to check.

This guide explains a practical daily sales record book format for small businesses, with examples you can adapt to your products, services, currency, and local requirements. For the full recording process, see [How to Record Daily Sales for a Small Business](/blog/how-to-record-daily-sales-small-business).

## 1. Choose the right level of detail

There are two common ways to organize a sales register.

**Transaction-level register:** one row for each sale, with the total amount, customer, and payment status. This is quick for simple transactions.

**Item-level register:** one row for each product or service line, with a shared sale reference for items sold together. This makes it easier to review product quantities and connect sales to stock records.

A shop selling several products in one transaction should usually keep item-level detail somewhere, even if it also maintains a transaction summary. Otherwise, the day's total may show how much was sold without showing which products moved.

## 2. Daily sales book columns

A practical transaction register can use these columns:

| Column          | What to record                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Date            | Date the sale occurred                                 |
| Sale reference  | Unique receipt, invoice, or transaction number         |
| Customer        | Customer name or a walk-in label, where appropriate    |
| Description     | Product, service, or a reference to the itemized lines |
| Subtotal        | Amount before discounts and applicable taxes           |
| Discount        | Approved discount, if any                              |
| Tax             | Applicable tax amount, where required                  |
| Sale total      | Final amount charged                                   |
| Amount received | Payments recorded against the sale                     |
| Balance         | Sale total less amount received                        |
| Payment method  | Cash, bank transfer, card, or other method             |
| Status          | Paid, partly paid, or unpaid                           |

A business may need additional fields such as salesperson, location, order number, delivery reference, or currency. Add fields that answer a real operational question rather than making every row difficult to complete.

## 3. Example of a completed daily sales record

The following is an illustrative example only. It is not a real business transaction or a suggested tax calculation.

| Date   | Reference | Description   | Sale total | Received | Balance | Status      |
| ------ | --------- | ------------- | ---------: | -------: | ------: | ----------- |
| 27 Nov | SALE-014  | Water bottles | $24.00     | $24.00   | $0.00   | Paid        |
| 27 Nov | SALE-015  | Notebooks     | $20.00     | $5.00    | $15.00  | Partly paid |
| 27 Nov | SALE-016  | Pen sets      | $15.00     | $0.00    | $15.00  | Unpaid      |

This register shows $59.00 in sales, $29.00 received, and $30.00 still outstanding in the example. The sales total and the amount received are different measures; do not report them as though they were the same.

When a customer pays the remaining $15.00 later, add the receipt against SALE-015. Keep the original sale date and record the later payment using its actual receipt date.

## 4. Keep multi-product sales itemized

When one customer buys several products, use one sale reference and record a separate line for each item.

| Sale reference | Product      | Quantity | Unit price | Line total |
| -------------- | ------------ | -------: | ---------: | ---------: |
| SALE-017       | Water bottle | 2        | $8.00      | $16.00     |
| SALE-017       | Notebook     | 3        | $4.00      | $12.00     |
| SALE-017       | Pen set      | 1        | $5.00      | $5.00      |

In this illustrative example, SALE-017 has a subtotal of $33.00 before any applicable discounts or taxes.

This structure preserves the quantity sold for each product. It also prevents a common spreadsheet error: counting the same transaction total once for every product line.

## 5. How to total the day correctly

At the end of the day, calculate and review separate figures:

- **Sales recorded:** totals of completed sales for the chosen date.
- **Payments received:** payments actually recorded on that date, including payments for earlier sales where relevant.
- **Outstanding balances:** unpaid portions of sales, using the business's proper receivables records.
- **Refunds and adjustments:** separately identified corrections, returns, and cancellations.

Do not subtract expenses from sales and call the result cash received. Profit, cash flow, and sales are different concepts. For financial reporting, use the accounting records and reports that apply to the business.

## 6. Paper sales book or digital register?

A paper sales book can be appropriate where transaction volume is low and one person is responsible for keeping it. Number pages or receipts consistently, write legibly, and keep supporting receipts and invoices in an orderly place.

A spreadsheet can calculate totals and make searching easier, but protect formulas, control access, and make backups. A spreadsheet does not automatically connect sales to payments or stock.

Accounting software may be useful when several people record transactions, customer balances need follow-up, or sales should connect with invoicing and accounting. Review the features and plan limits before moving your records.

## 7. A simple closing routine

Before closing for the day:

1. Check that each completed sale has a reference and date.
2. Confirm that item quantities and prices are reasonable.
3. Review paid, partly paid, and unpaid status.
4. Compare recorded cash receipts with the cash count, accounting for documented cash movements.
5. Compare electronic receipts with the relevant transaction records or statements.
6. Check unusual differences, refunds, and cancelled sales.
7. Keep the supporting documents with the records they relate to.

A difference between the sales register and the money counted should be investigated. Do not change transaction amounts just to make the register agree with the cash drawer.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best format for a daily sales book?

Use a format that records the date, reference, description, amount charged, amount received, balance, and payment status. Product sellers should also preserve quantities and unit prices for each item.

### Should I record a sale if the customer has not paid?

Yes. Record the sale and its outstanding balance using the appropriate sales and receivables process. Record the later payment separately when it is received.

### Should cash sales and credit sales be recorded together?

They may appear in the same sales register, but their payment statuses must remain clear. A sales register records the sale; a cash book or payment record shows when money was received.

### How often should I total the sales book?

Review the register daily where practical. Also review it over weekly and monthly periods so that unusual patterns, missing records, and unresolved balances can be investigated.
