# Invoice Payment Terms: Examples, Net 30, and How to Choose

An invoice tells a customer how much they owe. **Invoice payment terms explain when payment is due, whether an early-payment discount applies, and what the customer should do to settle the balance.**

Clear terms help both sides plan. A customer can route an invoice through the right approval process, while the business issuing it can estimate when money may arrive and follow up consistently.

This guide explains common payment terms, shows how to calculate due dates, compares options such as Net 15 and Net 30, and provides wording examples you can adapt to an agreement. The examples are illustrative, not legal or accounting advice.

## What are invoice payment terms?

Invoice payment terms are the agreed conditions for paying an invoice. They usually describe:

- **When payment is due**, such as immediately, within 15 days, or by a named calendar date
- **How payment is calculated**, including whether the clock starts on the invoice date, delivery, acceptance, or another agreed event
- **Whether an early-payment discount applies**
- **Whether payment is split** into a deposit and later installments or milestones
- **What payment instructions** the customer should follow
- **What happens if payment is late**, where this has been agreed and is appropriate

Payment terms should not be a surprise added after work is complete. Discuss them during quoting, onboarding, or contract negotiation, then make the invoice consistent with what both parties accepted.

A short label such as "Net 30" can be useful, but a specific due date is easier to act on. When possible, show both the agreed term and the calendar date.

## Common invoice payment terms explained

The right term depends on the work, customer relationship, project risk, industry practice, and the amount of time your business can wait for payment. The following are common commercial examples, not rules that every business must use.

| Payment term        | Typical meaning                                                                                             | Where it may be considered                                                             |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Due upon receipt    | Payment is expected when the customer receives the invoice                                                  | Small one-off jobs or agreed immediate-payment transactions                            |
| Net 7               | Payment is due within 7 days of the agreed starting date                                                    | Short projects or customers who accept a short payment cycle                           |
| Net 15              | Payment is due within 15 days                                                                               | Freelancers and service providers who want a shorter collection window                 |
| Net 30              | Payment is due within 30 days                                                                               | Business-to-business work where the parties agree to a monthly payment window          |
| Net 60 / Net 90     | Payment is due within 60 or 90 days                                                                         | Larger procurement processes or negotiated customer arrangements                       |
| 2/10 Net 30         | A 2% discount is available if the customer pays within 10 days; otherwise the full amount is due in 30 days | Businesses that have assessed whether an early-payment discount makes commercial sense |
| Deposit and balance | Part is paid before work starts and the remainder at an agreed milestone or completion point                | Projects with setup costs, materials, or staged delivery                               |
| Milestone payments  | Payment is split across named project stages                                                                | Longer assignments with several deliverables                                           |
| End of month (EOM)  | Payment is due at an agreed month-end date                                                                  | Customers that use scheduled monthly payment runs                                      |

Terms such as EOM can be interpreted differently across agreements. Do not rely on an acronym alone: write the actual date or explain exactly how the date is determined.

### What does Net 30 mean?

Net 30 generally means the full invoice amount is due within 30 days of the date or event specified in the agreement. Many business invoices count from the invoice date, but the parties should confirm the starting point and any exceptions.

For example, if an invoice is dated November 22, 2026 and the agreed terms are Net 30 from the invoice date, the illustrative due date is December 22, 2026.

The word "Net" refers to the amount due after any agreed discounts or adjustments. It does not mean that a customer automatically receives 30 business days. Unless the agreement says otherwise, businesses commonly use calendar days for net terms. State the actual due date so neither party has to guess.

### What does 2/10 Net 30 mean?

"2/10 Net 30" is commonly used to describe a 2% discount when the customer pays within 10 days, with the full balance due within 30 days if the discount is not used.

**Example:** An invoice is $5,000 and the parties agree to 2/10 Net 30.

- If the customer qualifies for the discount and pays within the agreed 10-day window, the amount would be $4,900.
- If the customer does not take the discount, the full $5,000 is due by the agreed Net 30 date.

The business should calculate the cost of the discount before offering it. Receiving money earlier may help with cash planning, but the discount also reduces the amount collected. Confirm the exact qualifying dates and conditions in the written agreement.

## Invoice payment terms examples you can adapt

These examples are starting points for clear communication. Replace the bracketed details, confirm that the wording matches the agreement, and use only terms your business is prepared to honor.

### 1. Due upon receipt

> Payment is due upon receipt of this invoice. Please use the payment instructions provided below. If you have a question about the invoice, contact [business contact] before the due date.

This wording is direct, but it should only be used when immediate payment has already been agreed. It should not be introduced unexpectedly after the customer has accepted a different schedule.

### 2. Net 15

> Payment is due within 15 calendar days of the invoice date. Invoice date: [date]. Payment due date: [specific date].

Including both dates makes the deadline visible. If your agreement uses a different starting event or day-count convention, change the wording to match it.

### 3. Net 30

> Payment is due within 30 calendar days of the invoice date. Invoice date: [date]. Payment due date: [specific date].

For a customer with an internal approval process, also confirm whether the invoice needs a purchase order number, project reference, named approver, or a particular submission address.

### 4. Deposit and final balance

> A deposit of [percentage or amount] is due before work begins. The remaining balance is due [at the agreed milestone / when the agreed work is completed], as described in the project agreement.

**Example:** For a $2,000 project with an agreed 50% deposit, the first payment is $1,000 and the remaining $1,000 is due at the agreed completion milestone. This is an illustrative split, not a recommended rate for every project.

### 5. Milestone payments

> Payment is divided into the following agreed milestones: [milestone 1 and amount], [milestone 2 and amount], and [final milestone and amount]. Each invoice will identify the milestone covered and its due date.

Milestone wording is especially useful when the customer is paying for distinct stages rather than one delivery at the end.

### 6. Early-payment discount

> A [discount percentage] discount applies if the full eligible balance is received by [specific date]. Otherwise, the undiscounted balance of [amount] is due by [final due date].

Write the discount amount, qualifying date, and final due date clearly. Avoid vague wording such as "discount for quick payment."

### 7. Payment by a named date

> Please pay the amount shown on this invoice no later than [day, month, year]. If your payment process requires a purchase order or additional reference, please contact [business contact] promptly.

A named date can reduce confusion when the agreement uses a custom schedule rather than standard net terms.

## How to calculate an invoice due date

Start with the date or event specified in the written agreement. If the agreement says that the clock starts on the invoice date, add the agreed number of days to that date.

For a simple illustration, assume an invoice is issued on November 22, 2026:

| Agreed term | Illustrative due date |
| ----------- | --------------------- |
| Net 7       | November 29, 2026     |
| Net 15      | December 7, 2026      |
| Net 30      | December 22, 2026     |
| Net 60      | January 21, 2027      |

These dates assume calendar-day counting from the invoice date and no special weekend, holiday, receipt, or contract rule. Your agreement and applicable requirements may define a different calculation. If the date falls on a weekend or holiday, do not assume it automatically moves: check the agreed wording and confirm with the customer.

A practical invoice review should check:

1. The invoice date is correct.
2. The agreed payment term is shown accurately.
3. The due date follows the agreed starting point and counting method.
4. Any discount or installment is calculated correctly.
5. The invoice includes the customer's required reference or purchase order details.
6. The payment instructions match the arrangement already agreed.

## Net 15 vs. Net 30: how should a small business choose?

Neither term is universally better. A shorter term may bring the expected payment date forward, but a customer may need more time to approve the invoice. A longer term may fit a customer's procurement process, but it leaves the business waiting longer for the cash.

Consider these questions before agreeing to a term:

### 1. How predictable is the customer's payment process?

Ask who approves invoices, when payment runs happen, and what documentation is required. A clear process can matter as much as the number of days printed on the invoice.

### 2. Can your business cover costs while waiting?

Think about the time between completing the work and receiving payment. Consider supplier bills, contractor costs, rent, subscriptions, and other operating expenses that fall due during that period.

### 3. Is the customer relationship established?

A new customer and a long-standing customer may present different levels of uncertainty. Your business can set a consistent policy while still agreeing on terms that reflect the transaction and the customer's process.

### 4. Does the project require upfront spending?

If materials, travel, or outside services must be paid before delivery, discuss a deposit or milestone arrangement before accepting the work. Make sure each stage and payment trigger is clear.

### 5. Is the discount worth its cost?

An early-payment discount reduces the amount collected. Compare that cost with the value of receiving the money earlier and the effect on your own cash needs.

### 6. Are the terms clear and mutually accepted?

Record the agreed terms in the proposal, contract, purchase order, or other appropriate written record. The invoice should repeat them accurately rather than introduce new conditions.

## What to include in an invoice payment terms section

A useful payment section is specific enough for a customer to act without asking what the business means. Check whether it includes:

- **The agreed term:** for example, Net 15 or a named milestone
- **Invoice date:** the date the invoice was issued
- **Payment due date:** a calendar date where possible
- **Amount due:** the full amount or the installment currently payable
- **Discount conditions:** if an early-payment discount was agreed
- **Payment instructions:** the correct method and reference information
- **Contact for questions:** a person or business email that can resolve invoice queries
- **Late-payment wording:** only where it has been agreed and is appropriate for the transaction and jurisdiction

If a customer uses purchase orders, vendor portals, or scheduled payment runs, confirm those requirements before sending the invoice. A technically correct invoice can still be delayed if it is sent to the wrong place or is missing a required reference.

## Common payment terms mistakes to avoid

### Using an acronym without explaining it

Not every customer uses the same terms every day. Write a short explanation and include a calendar due date.

### Changing the terms after work is complete

Unexpected changes can damage trust and create disputes. Discuss and document payment terms before the transaction or project begins.

### Confusing invoice date with delivery or acceptance date

Some agreements count from the invoice date; others use receipt, delivery, approval, or another event. State the agreed starting point.

### Offering discounts without checking the numbers

An early-payment discount affects revenue collected. Calculate the amount and make sure the business understands the trade-off before offering it.

### Adding unclear late-fee language

Late charges may be subject to contract terms and local rules. Do not assume that a fee is automatically permitted or enforceable. Confirm the applicable requirements before using such wording.

### Forgetting to update the due date after a correction

If an invoice is corrected or reissued, make sure the invoice date, due date, reference number, and customer communication remain consistent with the agreed arrangement.

## How to manage invoices after sending them

Setting a clear term is only the beginning. A small business also needs a repeatable way to see which invoices are open, which have been paid, and which need follow-up.

A simple process can include:

1. Save a copy of every issued invoice.
2. Record the invoice date, due date, customer, amount, and reference.
3. Review outstanding invoices on a regular schedule.
4. Confirm whether a payment has arrived before sending a reminder.
5. Match received payments to the correct invoice.
6. Investigate short payments, duplicate records, disputes, and missing references.
7. Review overdue balances and update cash-flow expectations.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are standard invoice payment terms?

Common examples include due upon receipt, Net 7, Net 15, Net 30, Net 60, deposits, milestone payments, and early-payment discounts. The appropriate term depends on the transaction and what both parties agree.

### Is Net 30 30 calendar days or business days?

Net 30 commonly means 30 calendar days, but the agreement should state the starting point and any exceptions. Include a specific due date to avoid confusion.

### Does Net 30 start on the invoice date?

Often it does, but not always. The contract or purchase agreement may define the start as the date an invoice is received, approved, or another event. Follow the written agreement.

### Is Net 15 better than Net 30?

Not in every situation. Net 15 brings the expected due date forward, while Net 30 gives the customer a longer payment window. Consider your costs, customer process, and cash-flow needs before agreeing.

### Can a small business ask for a deposit?

A business can propose a deposit or milestone schedule when negotiating a project. The amount, timing, refund conditions, and work covered should be agreed clearly before the project begins.

### Should I put late fees on an invoice?

Do not add a late fee automatically. Any late-payment charge should be agreed appropriately and checked against the rules that apply to the transaction. Seek qualified advice if you are unsure.

### What is the difference between payment terms and a due date?

Payment terms describe the agreed payment conditions, such as Net 30 or a 50% deposit followed by a final payment. The due date is the specific calendar deadline for an amount currently payable.

### Can I change payment terms after sending an invoice?

Do not assume you can change them unilaterally. Contact the customer, reach agreement on any change, and keep the written record and invoice consistent with what both parties accept.
