# Daily Sales Report for a Small Business: Template and Example

A daily sales report gives a business owner a clear summary of what happened during a trading day: how much was sold, which products or services contributed to sales, how customers paid, what remains unpaid, and whether the recorded cash agrees with the amount counted.

It is different from a daily sales register. The register captures individual transactions. The report summarizes those transactions so the owner can review performance and spot problems without reading every line.

This guide explains how to prepare a daily sales report for a small business, what fields to include, how to separate sales from money received, and how to build a practical report in a spreadsheet.

## What Is a Daily Sales Report?

A daily sales report is a dated summary of completed sales activity for a business, store, sales channel, or shift. It turns individual sales records into a concise view of the day.

A useful report can answer five questions:

1. How much did the business sell?
2. Which products, services, or categories contributed to the total?
3. How much money was actually received, and through which payment methods?
4. How much remains unpaid or needs follow-up?
5. Were there returns, discounts, cash differences, or other exceptions to review?

A report may be prepared at the end of each trading day, or at the end of each shift when a business has several shifts or locations. The right format depends on the number of transactions and how the business operates.

## Daily Sales Report Template: What to Include

A practical report should be short enough to complete consistently but detailed enough to explain the totals. Start with these sections.

### 1. Report details

Record the business name, report date, location or sales channel, shift if relevant, and the person who prepared or reviewed the report. If the business works in more than one currency, identify the currency on every report and do not combine different currencies into one total.

### 2. Sales summary

Include the figures that explain how the day's sales total was calculated.

| Field | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Gross sales | Value of completed sales before discounts and returns, using one consistent definition |
| Discounts | Discounts applied to those sales |
| Returns and refunds | Value of goods returned or sales reversed during the reporting period |
| Net sales | Gross sales minus discounts and returns, before separately tracked sales tax |
| Sales tax or similar taxes | Tax collected or charged, shown separately where applicable |
| Customer charges | The amount customers were charged after discounts, returns, and applicable taxes |

Businesses may use different definitions for gross and net sales in their point-of-sale or accounting systems. State the definition used in the report and apply it consistently. Do not compare two reports that calculate these fields differently.

### 3. Payment summary

Show how much money was received during the day, separated by payment method. Common categories include cash, card, bank transfer, mobile money, online payment services, and other methods accepted by the business.

Keep unpaid or credit sales separate from money already collected. A customer paying later does not change the date on which the original sale occurred. It creates a receipt on the later payment date.

### 4. Product or service breakdown

Summarize sales by product, service, or category. For a small shop, the report might show the quantity sold and the sales value for each product. A service business might group work by service type or project category instead.

This section helps the owner see which items contributed most to the day's activity. It also gives the business a starting point for checking stock movements where inventory is tracked.

### 5. Checks and notes

Add a small section for exceptions: cash differences, unusual refunds, cancelled transactions, missing payment references, stock discrepancies, or unpaid customer balances that require follow-up.

A report should explain exceptions rather than silently changing the totals to make them match.

## A Daily Sales Report Example

The following example is illustrative. It shows how a small product shop might summarize one trading day. The amounts are invented and are not actual FinFlow Track customer data.

### Example sales summary

| Report field | Example amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Gross sales | $2,500.00 |
| Discounts | $150.00 |
| Returns | $100.00 |
| Net sales before tax | $2,250.00 |
| Sales tax collected | $90.00 |
| Customer charges after adjustments | $2,340.00 |

In this example, net sales are calculated as gross sales minus discounts and returns. Tax is shown separately rather than being treated as sales revenue. The exact labels and tax presentation should follow the business's accounting setup and local requirements.

### Example payment summary

| Payment or balance category | Example amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Cash received | $800.00 |
| Card, bank, and mobile payments received | $1,100.00 |
| Total payments received | $1,900.00 |
| Unpaid customer balance from sales in the report | $440.00 |
| Total customer charges | $2,340.00 |

The example shows why sales and receipts must be reported separately. Customer charges total $2,340, but only $1,900 has been received so far. The remaining $440 is unpaid at the time of the report. It should not be included in the cash counted or treated as a completed payment.

If a customer makes a partial payment, record the amount received and the remaining balance separately. When the rest arrives later, record the receipt against the original invoice or sale reference.

### Example product breakdown

| Product | Quantity sold | Net sales value |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Water bottle | 30 | $240.00 |
| Notebook | 55 | $220.00 |
| Phone charger | 20 | $300.00 |
| Other products | — | $1,490.00 |
| **Total** | **Example total** | **$2,250.00** |

The item values above are illustrative and are grouped to show the layout. In a real report, the product breakdown should reconcile to the net sales total under the same definitions, date range, and currency.

## How to Prepare a Daily Sales Report Step by Step

### Step 1: Collect the day's source records

Start with the records that support each transaction: sales register entries, invoices, order confirmations, receipts, cash register summaries, and payment-provider or bank transaction reports where available.

For US businesses, the IRS lists examples of supporting records for gross receipts, including cash register tapes, deposit information for cash and credit sales, receipt books, and invoices. This is US recordkeeping guidance; businesses in other countries should follow the requirements that apply in their own jurisdiction. See the [IRS guide to business recordkeeping documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/what-kind-of-records-should-i-keep).

### Step 2: Confirm which transactions belong in the report

Use a consistent cut-off time and include completed sales for the selected date or shift. Keep quotes, drafts, abandoned carts, and cancelled orders out of completed-sales totals. Record refunds, returns, and corrections in the appropriate adjustment fields rather than deleting the original history where the system supports an audit trail.

If an order was placed before midnight but paid or fulfilled later, use the date and accounting treatment defined by the business's process. Avoid switching between order date, invoice date, and payment date without identifying which date each report is using.

### Step 3: Summarize sales by product, service, and channel

Group completed transactions by the categories that are useful to the business. A shop may want a product breakdown. A service provider may want sales by service type. A business selling in person and online may need separate channel totals.

Do not add together figures from different currencies. Prepare separate totals for each currency or use a documented conversion process in the accounting records.

### Step 4: Calculate discounts, returns, taxes, and net sales

Show how the sales total was derived. Keep discounts and returns visible instead of recording only a final figure. Where sales tax, VAT, or another transaction tax applies, follow the business's local accounting and tax treatment and show the tax separately where appropriate.

The report is an operational summary, not a substitute for professional accounting or tax advice.

### Step 5: Separate payments received from unpaid sales

Summarize amounts actually received by payment method. Keep unpaid balances and partial payments visible, and identify the original sale or invoice reference.

A payment-provider notification, card receipt, or transfer reference can help with matching. It should still be checked against the relevant account or provider records during reconciliation.

For more detail on customer balances and follow-up, see [Accounts Receivable Management for Small Business](/blog/accounts-receivable-management).

### Step 6: Reconcile the cash drawer and electronic receipts

For a cash drawer, one useful control is to compare the expected closing cash with the amount physically counted.

A basic calculation is:

~~~text
Expected closing cash =
  Opening cash float
  + Cash received from customers
  - Cash refunds paid from the drawer
  - Approved cash expenses paid from the drawer
  - Cash removed from the drawer
~~~

Compare the expected amount with the actual cash count. Record and investigate any difference. Do not include card, bank transfer, or mobile payments in the physical cash total.

Reconcile electronic receipts separately with bank statements or payment-provider reports. Settlement timing, processing fees, reversals, and grouped deposits can make the amount shown on a sales screen different from the amount that reaches the bank account. Record fees and timing differences using the appropriate bookkeeping process rather than changing the original sale total.

### Step 7: Review the report and assign follow-up actions

Before closing the report, check for missing transactions, duplicate entries, unusual discounts, refunds without explanations, unpaid sales, and missing payment references. Assign a person and a due date to any follow-up that cannot be completed immediately.

Keep the report and its supporting records together so the totals can be checked later.

## How to Create a Daily Sales Report in Excel or Google Sheets

A spreadsheet is often enough for a small business with a manageable volume of transactions. Use two sheets rather than trying to place every transaction and every summary figure in one large table.

### Sheet 1: Sales transactions

Use one row per completed sale or one row per product line, with a shared sale reference when one transaction contains multiple products.

Suggested columns:

| Date | Sale reference | Product or service | Quantity | Unit price | Discount | Tax | Line total | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 27 Nov | SALE-001 | Water bottle | 3 | $8.00 | $0.00 | $0.00 | $24.00 | Complete |
| 27 Nov | SALE-002 | Notebook | 5 | $4.00 | $0.00 | $0.00 | $20.00 | Complete |

These are illustrative sample entries. Use the fields and tax treatment appropriate to your business. If one sale includes several products, repeat the sale reference on each product line and make sure the report does not count the transaction total more than once.

### Sheet 2: Payments received

Use one row per payment, not one row per sale. This allows a customer to pay in more than one installment.

Suggested columns:

| Payment date | Sale reference | Payment method | Payment reference | Amount received | Currency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 27 Nov | SALE-001 | Cash | — | $24.00 | USD |
| 28 Nov | SALE-002 | Bank transfer | TXN-EXAMPLE-02 | $20.00 | USD |

The second example payment is dated the following day to demonstrate that the sale date and payment date can be different. It is illustrative only.

### Sheet 3: Daily summary

Create one row per reporting date, then calculate the totals from the underlying sheets. In Excel, SUMIFS can add values that meet multiple conditions, such as a selected date and payment method. Microsoft explains the function and its syntax in its [official SUMIFS documentation](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/functions/sumifs-function).

For example, if the payment sheet has the payment date in column A, method in column C, and amount in column E, a formula to total cash received for the report date in cell A2 is:

~~~excel
=SUMIFS(Payments!$E:$E,Payments!$A:$A,$A2,Payments!$C:$C,"Cash")
~~~

Adjust the sheet names and column references to match your own workbook. Keep formulas protected from accidental editing, use clear column headings, and test the totals against a few transactions before relying on the report.

A spreadsheet can calculate totals, but it cannot confirm that a cash count, bank deposit, or payment-provider settlement is correct. Those checks still require the underlying records.

## Daily Sales Report vs. Sales Register vs. Cash Book

These records are related, but they serve different purposes.

| Record | Main purpose | Typical detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales register | Captures individual sales | Date, reference, product, quantity, price, customer, status |
| Daily sales report | Summarizes trading activity | Daily totals, product breakdown, payment summary, exceptions |
| Cash book | Records cash and bank movements | Receipts, payments, deposits, withdrawals, balances |

A daily report should be traceable back to the sales register and payment records. It should not replace the detailed transaction history or the formal accounting records required by the business.

## Common Daily Sales Reporting Mistakes

### Counting unpaid sales as money received

A completed sale and a collected payment are not always the same event. Show the outstanding amount separately and update it when payment arrives.

### Combining different payment channels

Cash, card, bank transfer, and mobile payments need separate totals. This makes reconciliation easier and helps explain why the till total differs from the bank deposit.

### Treating sales tax as business revenue

Transaction taxes may be collected from customers but are not necessarily revenue belonging to the business. Follow the accounting treatment that applies locally and show the amount clearly in the report.

### Hiding discounts, returns, or cancellations

Keep adjustments visible. A final total without supporting details is harder to review and investigate.

### Reporting only one total for a multi-product shop

A daily total does not show which products sold or whether stock records need attention. Add a short product or category breakdown where it helps the business make decisions.

### Changing the report to force a cash match

A cash difference should be documented and investigated. Changing sales entries without evidence can make future reports less reliable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a daily sales report include?

Include the report date, gross sales, discounts, returns, net sales, applicable taxes, customer charges, payments received by method, unpaid balances, transaction count, product or service totals, and any exceptions that need review.

### How do you calculate daily net sales?

A common operational calculation is gross sales minus discounts and returns for the reporting period. Keep sales tax separate where appropriate. Confirm the definitions used by your accounting system, because reporting conventions can differ.

### Is a daily sales report the same as a daily sales book?

No. A sales book or register records individual transactions. A daily sales report summarizes those transactions for review. The report should be traceable to the detailed register.

### Should credit sales appear in a daily sales report?

Yes, but show them separately from money received. Record the completed sale and identify the unpaid balance. When the customer pays later, record the receipt on its payment date and link it to the original sale or invoice.

### Can I prepare a daily sales report in Excel?

Yes. A spreadsheet can work for a small business with a manageable transaction volume. Keep transaction details and payment receipts in separate tables, use formulas carefully, protect important cells, and reconcile the totals with actual cash and electronic payment records.

### How often should a small business prepare a sales report?

Prepare it at the end of each trading day when daily visibility is useful. A business with multiple shifts or locations may need a report for each shift or location, followed by a combined daily summary. The process should match the business's transaction volume and control needs.

## Final Thoughts

A useful daily sales report is more than a number at the bottom of a page. It explains what the business sold, how much was collected, what remains unpaid, and whether the day's records agree with cash and electronic payment evidence.

Start with a consistent format, keep sales separate from receipts, show adjustments clearly, and review exceptions before they become difficult to trace. A paper register or spreadsheet can be a practical starting point. As transaction volume and team responsibilities grow, structured sales and accounting records can make the reporting process easier to maintain.
